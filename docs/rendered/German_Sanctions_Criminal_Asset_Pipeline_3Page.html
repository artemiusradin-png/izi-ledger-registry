Germany: Criminally Confiscated Assets Pathway for Ukraine

Operational Playbook (Finalized Confiscation Cases Only)

Author
Affiliation

Artem Radin

Intern

Published

February 20, 2026

ImportantStrict Scope

This paper is limited to criminal cases with finalized confiscation/forfeiture in Germany (assets legally transferred to the state after criminal proceedings).

Excluded: assets only frozen/blocked under sanctions without a final criminal confiscation outcome.

ImportantPractical Objective

Determine how Ukraine can lawfully obtain value from assets already confiscated by Germany in criminal proceedings, and what can be done immediately versus what needs legal/policy change.

1 Who Can Actually File (Plain Answer)

1.1 Can the think tank file claims itself?

Usually no. A think tank generally has no standing unless it is itself the direct injured owner/claimant.

1.2 Who should file instead?

Actor Can file claim for confiscated proceeds? Typical role
Government of Ukraine (authorized body) Yes, where legal entitlement is shown Primary public claimant
Ukrainian ministry/agency Yes, if it has legal mandate and victim link Sector claimant (state property/public funds)
Ukrainian SOE Yes, if direct victim/owner Corporate claimant
Private victim (UA entity/person) Yes, if directly harmed Individual/corporate restitution claimant
German prosecutor No, not as Ukraine’s representative Prosecutes crime, seeks confiscation ex officio
Think tank Normally no Case development, evidence package, coordination

1.3 Practical operating model for the think tank

  1. Select candidate finalized cases and identify the legally strongest claimant.
  2. Build the evidence dossier and litigation memo (ownership, tracing, damage, legal theory).
  3. Transfer filing package to authorized claimant + German counsel.
  4. Track deadlines, appeals status, and enforcement distribution stage.
  5. Run parallel policy track for state-to-state transfer where claimant route is weak.

3 “Lawsuit Mechanism” - Operational Steps

3.1 Standing (Who can initiate)

  • Ukraine as a state (through authorized governmental body)
  • Ukrainian ministries/agencies with legal personality/mandate
  • State-owned entities (if direct victim/owner)
  • Other directly injured Ukrainian legal or natural persons
  • Prosecutors act ex officio for confiscation, not as Ukraine’s private counsel
  • Think tanks/research NGOs generally lack standing unless they are direct victims

3.2 Competent Forum / Authority

  • Criminal court for confiscation order
  • Prosecutor’s office during investigation/prosecution phase
  • Enforcement authority for distribution/liquidation stage
  • Civil court if separate civil claim is chosen
  • Federal vs Land level depends on prosecutorial/court jurisdiction and asset destination in budget system

3.3 Filing Mechanics

  • Written filing to competent court/authority; structured legal application
  • German language (certified translation for supporting foreign documents)
  • Representation by counsel admitted in Germany strongly required for effectiveness
  • Service via court channels (electronic filing where enabled)
  • Court/legal fees depend on procedural route and value in dispute

3.4 Evidentiary Package

  • Proof of ownership/beneficial ownership
  • Proof linking asset to predicate crime (including sanctions-evasion conduct where relevant)
  • Victimhood and quantified damage
  • Asset tracing and chain-of-custody materials
  • Corporate records, banking records, transaction data
  • Expert reports (forensics, valuation, tracing)

3.5 Timeline and Decision Points

  1. Seizure order in criminal investigation
  2. Indictment and trial
  3. Confiscation order
  4. Appeals/finality
  5. Enforcement and liquidation
  6. Distribution decision (victim restitution and/or public budget route)
  7. Potential transfer mechanism toward Ukraine

3.6 Typical Obstacles / Defenses

  • Due process and proportionality objections
  • Third-party rights and competing ownership claims
  • Tracing failures (layered structures, nominees, offshore chains)
  • Insufficient proof of direct victim entitlement
  • Procedural timing misses and formal defects

4 Action Checklist (“How to Proceed”)

ImportantObjective

Obtain payment/return from already confiscated assets in Germany through the victim/claimant route, and use a governmental route if claimant standing is unavailable.

4.1 Step-by-step execution plan

Step Stage
1 Go/No-Go gate
2 Claimant selection
3 Counsel and strategy
4 Evidence package
5 File claim
6 Adversarial phase
7 Secure payout
8 Fallback track (parallel)
  1. Screen the case for legal eligibility (Go/No-Go gate).
    • Confirm there is a final criminal confiscation order (finality reached after appeal period/appeals).
    • Confirm the target is confiscated property/proceeds, not only frozen sanctioned assets.
    • Identify which authority holds the file at enforcement stage (court/prosecutor/enforcement office).
  2. Select the legally strongest claimant.
    • Pick one primary claimant: Ukraine state body, ministry/agency, SOE, or direct private victim.
    • Obtain formal mandate documents (authorizing act, power of attorney, signatory proof).
    • If the think tank has no standing, position it as technical support only.
  3. Instruct German counsel and lock procedural strategy.
    • Engage counsel admitted in Germany for criminal-enforcement filings.
    • Choose route: restitution from confiscated proceeds, adhesion/civil add-on, or both.
    • Build a deadline calendar tied to notification dates and enforcement milestones.
  4. Build the minimum viable evidence package (without this, filing fails).
    • Ownership/beneficial ownership proof (registries, contracts, account records).
    • Tracing file linking confiscated asset/proceeds to claimant loss.
    • Crime linkage evidence (sanctions-evasion/ML/corruption connection in case file).
    • Damage quantification with methodology and supporting documents.
    • Certified translations into German for all key documents.
  5. File the claim in the correct forum and format.
    • Submit written application through counsel to the competent court/enforcement authority.
    • Include: legal basis, requested amount/asset, evidence index, and procedural requests.
    • Ensure service is completed correctly (electronic/registry channel as applicable).
  6. Actively manage adversarial phase.
    • Respond to objections: third-party rights, proportionality, causation, tracing gaps.
    • Cure evidentiary defects fast (supplemental declarations, expert tracing reports).
    • Track hearings/orders and keep claimant instructions updated.
  7. Secure enforcement payout/transfer execution.
    • After favorable decision, confirm payment order mechanics (account, currency, recipient legal identity).
    • Verify deductions/priority rules and net distributable amount.
    • Confirm treasury/enforcement processing timeline and written confirmation of transfer.
  8. Run fallback governmental track in parallel (risk control).
    • If claimant standing or tracing is weak, prepare state-to-state transfer proposal package.
    • Use finalized confiscation facts + beneficiary safeguards (auditing, ring-fencing, reporting).
    • Coordinate with authorized Ukrainian and German governmental counterparts.

4.2 Documents you should prepare immediately

  • Case-status memo proving final confiscation status
  • Claimant mandate pack (authority + POA + legal personality evidence)
  • Evidence index and chain-of-custody table
  • Asset tracing report (transaction map + ownership map)
  • Damages model (amount requested + calculation annexes)
  • German-language filing set (application + exhibits list + certified translations)
  • Deadline tracker (all statutory/procedural dates)

4.3 Practical success criteria

  • Correct claimant with proven standing
  • Direct linkage between confiscated value and claimant entitlement
  • On-time filing with complete German-language evidentiary set
  • Continuous enforcement follow-up until funds are actually transferred

5 Validated Case Studies (Framework-Mapped)

NoteFramework takeaways
  1. Germany has a working victim-claim route after final confiscation, but it is technically strict and evidence-driven.
  2. Cross-border transfers to victim populations usually require a second governmental agreement layer after confiscation.
  3. Successful models combine: final forfeiture + clear beneficiary design + monitored transfer mechanism.

UK → Nigeria (Ibori) UK prosecutors recovered; Nigeria as victim state UK–Nigeria agreement (9 Mar 2021) £4.2m returned for public-benefit projects Confiscation alone did not complete transfer; bilateral agreement operationalized destination
Switzerland → Nigeria (Abacha II) Swiss authorities post-criminal; Nigeria beneficiary 2017 Switzerland–Nigeria–World Bank tripartite agreement ~USD 321m restitution with monitoring safeguards Monitoring architecture reduces misuse risk, unlocks political/legal acceptance
Switzerland/Luxembourg → Peru State authorities via cooperation framework 2020 trilateral agreement; earlier ~USD 93m (2002–2006) ~USD 26m to justice-sector strengthening Restitution can be earmarked to rule-of-law infrastructure

Implication for Germany–Ukraine: Feasible now: victim/claimant route in finalized confiscation files (strict proof burden). Missing for scale: standing transfer framework converting German-confiscated value into structured Ukraine-directed disbursement. Best historical analogue: Swiss/UK style post-confiscation transfer agreement + safeguards.
