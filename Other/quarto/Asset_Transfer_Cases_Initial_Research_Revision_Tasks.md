# Revision Tasks for `Asset_Transfer_Cases_Initial_Research`

## Objective

Revise the current analysis so it answers the reviewer's request for:

- more **interstate asset recovery cases**;
- fuller **case-by-case analysis**;
- explicit coverage of the **accusation, parties, decision, legal basis for return, and post-return use of assets**;
- denser **source support** for follow-up research.

## Files in Scope

- `Other/quarto/Asset_Transfer_Cases_Initial_Research.qmd`
- `Other/quarto/Asset_Transfer_Cases_Initial_Research_UKR.qmd`

## Required Changes

### 1. Reframe the paper from "initial examples" to a structured interstate case study note

Task:
- Update the purpose and preliminary finding sections so the paper explicitly says it analyzes **interstate return mechanisms**, not only examples of transfer.

Expected result:
- The scope is clear from the first page.
- The reviewer immediately sees that the paper now focuses on **cross-border recovery and return architecture**.

### 2. Replace the current summary table with a fuller case matrix

Task:
- Expand the main table so each case includes:
  - accusation / predicate offense;
  - parties;
  - forum and decision date;
  - amount;
  - legal basis for confiscation;
  - legal basis for return to another state;
  - recipient/use of funds;
  - monitoring/joint project mechanism;
  - source links.

Expected result:
- The table becomes a usable index for deeper research.
- It should function as the high-level answer to the review comment.

### 3. Standardize every interstate case into the same analytical template

Task:
- For each interstate case, rewrite the section under the following subheadings:
  1. `Essence of the accusation`
  2. `Parties`
  3. `Procedural path and court decision`
  4. `Basis for return of assets`
  5. `Use of returned assets / joint projects`
  6. `Sources`

Cases that must be reworked first:
- Switzerland -> Nigeria (Abacha)
- UK -> Nigeria (Ibori)
- U.S. -> Malaysia (1MDB)

Expected result:
- All priority cases read in the same format.
- The paper stops looking like a narrative note and starts reading like legal case analysis.

### 4. Make the "basis for return" legally explicit

Task:
- For each interstate case, identify whether the return relied on:
  - bilateral treaty / MoU;
  - MLA framework;
  - domestic confiscation law;
  - UNCAC or another convention;
  - tripartite restitution agreement;
  - court order, settlement, or prosecutorial confiscation decision.
- State clearly which element authorized:
  - confiscation;
  - transfer abroad;
  - restrictions on use.

Expected result:
- The reader can distinguish the legal basis for **seizure/confiscation** from the legal basis for **actual repatriation**.

### 5. Add more interstate cases, not just the three current flagship examples

Task:
- Add at least 2 to 4 additional **verified interstate return cases**.
- Prioritize cases already hinted by existing sources in the draft:
  - Switzerland -> Kazakhstan
  - Switzerland/Luxembourg -> Peru
- Add only cases where the return itself, not just freezing, is documented.

Expected result:
- The paper shows a comparative pattern, not a three-case sample only.
- The new cases should be shorter than the flagship three if source material is thinner.

### 6. Separate "verified transfer" from "incomplete judicial record"

Task:
- Create a short verification note for each case:
  - `Transfer verified`
  - `Court decision verified`
  - `Return instrument verified`
  - `Use-of-funds evidence verified`
  - `Open gaps`

Expected result:
- Weaknesses are transparent.
- Missing court texts do not undermine stronger parts of the analysis.

### 7. Expand the post-return section on actual use of funds

Task:
- For each interstate case, state what happened after return:
  - general budget transfer or ring-fenced use;
  - monitored public project;
  - trust fund or social program;
  - evidence of implementation;
  - evidence of independent monitoring;
  - whether any joint project with an international organization was implemented.

Expected result:
- The paper answers the reviewer's question about the later fate of the assets.

### 8. Increase source density case by case

Task:
- Move from one or two sources per case to a source bundle:
  - official press release;
  - treaty / MoU / annex / agreement;
  - court decision or docket when available;
  - institutional monitoring note;
  - secondary institutional explainer only if needed.
- Add source links directly under each case section, not only in footnotes at the end.

Expected result:
- Each case can be independently checked without searching through the whole paper.

### 9. Tighten the domestic-vs-interstate split

Task:
- Keep domestic examples brief.
- State expressly that Madoff and Western Union are comparator models for **distribution mechanics**, but not interstate restitution cases.
- Move them to a shorter comparative section after the interstate part, or to an appendix if space is tight.

Expected result:
- The paper stays focused on the reviewer's request: interstate asset recovery.

Task:
- First complete the English revision in `Asset_Transfer_Cases_Initial_Research.qmd`.

## Concrete Deliverable Standard

The revised paper should let a reviewer answer these questions for each interstate case in under one minute:

- What was the accusation?
- Who were the parties?
- What decision or instrument made return possible?
- On what legal basis were assets returned abroad?
- How were the assets used afterward?
- Where are the underlying sources?
